Chess is a two-player strategy game with no dice and no luck. Each side starts with 16 pieces, and the goal is to **checkmate** the opposing king, which means attacking it so that it has no legal way to escape.

## Setting up the board

Place the board so that each player has a **light square in the bottom-right corner**. Rooks go in the corners, knights next to them, then bishops. The queen goes on the square of her own colour (white queen on a light square, black queen on a dark one), and the king takes the last square. All eight pawns line up on the second rank. White always moves first.

## The pieces and their values

Players use a simple points system to judge trades:

| Piece | Moves | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pawn | Forward one square (two on its first move), captures diagonally | 1 |
| Knight | L-shape, and the only piece that jumps | 3 |
| Bishop | Any distance diagonally | 3 |
| Rook | Any distance in a straight line | 5 |
| Queen | Any distance in any direction | 9 |
| King | One square in any direction | Priceless |

For a full breakdown of each piece, including castling, en passant and promotion, read our guide to [understanding chess pieces](/chess-game-understanding-chess-pieces).

## Tactics every player should know

Most games between beginners are decided by tactics, short sequences that win material. Learn to spot these patterns:

- **Fork**: one piece attacks two targets at once. Knights are famous for this.
- **Pin**: a piece can't move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.
- **Skewer**: the reverse of a pin, where the valuable piece is in front and must move, leaving the one behind to be captured.
- **Discovered attack**: moving one piece reveals an attack from another.
- **Back-rank mate**: a rook or queen checkmates a king that is trapped behind its own pawns.

## How to get better

Play regularly, go over your losses to find the move where things went wrong, and solve a few tactics puzzles every day. Learning the basic checkmates (king and queen against king, king and rook against king) pays off quickly.
