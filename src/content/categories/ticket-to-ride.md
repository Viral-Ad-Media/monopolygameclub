Ticket to Ride is a railway-building game for 2 to 5 players, designed by Alan R. Moon and first published in 2004. It won the Spiel des Jahres (German Game of the Year) the same year, and it remains one of the best "gateway" games for people new to modern board games. Players collect coloured train cards and use them to claim routes between cities on a map of North America.

## Setting up

Each player takes **45 plastic trains**, **4 train cards** and **3 destination tickets**, keeping at least two of the tickets. A destination ticket names two cities. Connect them with your own routes by the end of the game to score the points on the ticket. Fail, and you lose those points.

## On your turn, do one thing

1. **Draw train cards.** Take two, either from the five face-up cards or blind from the deck. Taking a face-up locomotive (wild card) counts as your whole draw.
2. **Claim a route.** Play a set of cards matching the colour and length of a route, then place your trains on it. Grey routes can be claimed with a set of any one colour.
3. **Draw destination tickets.** Take three and keep at least one.

## Scoring routes

| Route length | Points |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 6 | 15 |

The player with the **longest continuous path** of routes earns a 10-point bonus. When any player has two or fewer trains left, everyone, including that player, takes one final turn.

## Strategy tips

- **Long routes pay best.** A six-train route is worth 15 points, far more than six single routes.
- **Claim bottlenecks early.** Some cities have only one or two connections. If your tickets need them, take them before someone blocks you.
- **Don't hoard forever.** Collecting cards is safe, but others will claim the routes you need.
- **Draw new tickets late if you're ahead on trains.** Overlapping tickets that use routes you already own are free points.
