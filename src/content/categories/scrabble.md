Scrabble is the crossword game where every letter has a price. Two to four players draw letter tiles and build interlocking words on a 15 × 15 board, scoring points for each letter and earning more on premium squares. Architect Alfred Mosher Butts invented it in 1938 as *Criss-Crosswords*, and it took the name Scrabble in 1948.

## What's in the box

- A 15 × 15 board with premium squares
- **100 tiles**: 98 letters and 2 blanks, which can stand for any letter but score zero
- A rack for each player and a bag to draw from

## How to play

1. Everyone draws seven tiles. The player whose drawn tile is closest to A goes first.
2. The first word must cover the **centre star**, which counts as a double word score.
3. On each turn you either play a word, swap some tiles for new ones from the bag (and score nothing), or pass.
4. Every new word must read left to right or top to bottom and connect to tiles already on the board.
5. After playing, draw back up to seven tiles.

## Scoring

Add the letter values of every tile in each word you form. Premium squares only count on the turn a tile is first placed on them:

- **Double letter** and **triple letter** squares multiply a single tile.
- **Double word** and **triple word** squares multiply the whole word.
- Use all seven tiles in one turn and you get a **50-point bonus**, known as a bingo.

The game ends when the bag is empty and one player uses all their tiles, or when every player passes several turns in a row. Each player subtracts the value of their unplayed tiles. A player who went out adds everyone else's leftover tiles to their own score.

## Tips to score more

- **Learn the two-letter words.** Words like QI, ZA, XU, JO and KA let you squeeze high-value tiles into tight spots.
- **Know your Q words without a U**: QI, QAT, QADI, QOPH and FAQIR are all valid in common dictionaries.
- **Hunt for hooks.** Adding one letter to an existing word (CARE to SCARE, for example) lets you score two words at once.
- **Balance your rack.** Try to keep a mix of vowels and consonants. Save an S or a blank for a big play.
- **Don't open triple word squares** for your opponent unless you're getting plenty of points for it.
