Risk is the classic game of world conquest. It was invented by French film director Albert Lamorisse and released in 1957 as *La Conquête du Monde*. Two to six players command armies on a map of the world divided into **42 territories** across **six continents**, and the goal in the standard game is to eliminate every other player.

## Setting up

Players take turns claiming territories until the whole map is owned, then place their remaining armies on territories they hold. The number of starting armies depends on how many people are playing: the fewer players, the more armies each one gets.

## A turn has three steps

### 1. Reinforce

Count your territories and divide by three, rounding down. You get that many new armies, with a **minimum of three**. Add bonus armies for every whole continent you control:

| Continent | Territories | Bonus armies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asia | 12 | 7 |
| North America | 9 | 5 |
| Europe | 7 | 5 |
| Africa | 6 | 3 |
| South America | 4 | 2 |
| Australia | 4 | 2 |

You can also trade in sets of three Risk cards for extra armies. Each set traded in during the game is worth more than the last.

### 2. Attack

Attack from any territory with at least two armies into an adjacent enemy territory. The attacker rolls up to **three dice** (one fewer than the armies in the attacking territory, to a maximum of three) and the defender rolls up to **two**. Compare the highest dice of each side, then the second highest. The lower die loses one army each time, and **ties go to the defender**. Capture at least one territory in a turn and you earn a Risk card.

### 3. Fortify

At the end of your turn you may move armies from one of your territories to a connected one that you also own.

## Strategy tips

- **Start small.** Australia and South America are easy to defend because they have only one or two entry points. Holding one early gives you a steady bonus.
- **Don't spread thin.** A long line of single armies invites attacks. Keep your forces in a few strong stacks on your borders.
- **Grab a card every turn.** Even one small conquest earns a card, and card sets get more valuable as the game goes on.
- **Watch the leader.** Risk is a diplomatic game. When someone gets close to winning, the rest of the table should work together to break their continents.
- **Attack with numbers.** Attacking three dice against two gives the attacker a slight edge, so attack with big stacks and avoid long fights from small ones.
