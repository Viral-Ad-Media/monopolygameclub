Snakes and Ladders is a simple race game for two or more players, and it's often the first board game children learn. Roll the die, move your counter, climb the ladders and slide down the snakes. The first player to reach the last square wins.

The game began in ancient India, where versions known as *Moksha Patam* used the board to teach moral lessons: ladders stood for virtues and snakes for vices. It reached Britain in the late 1800s, and the American version, *Chutes and Ladders*, followed in 1943.
