Catan is a game about building, trading and a little bit of luck. Three or four players settle an island, collect resources from the land around their settlements and race to **10 victory points**. Here's how it works and how to win.

## Building the island

The board is made of **19 hexagonal tiles**:

- 3 hills (brick)
- 4 forests (lumber)
- 4 pastures (wool)
- 4 fields (grain)
- 3 mountains (ore)
- 1 desert (nothing)

Shuffle the tiles into a large hexagon, surround them with the sea frame and its harbours, then place a **number token** from 2 to 12 on every tile except the desert. The **robber** starts on the desert.

The dots under each number show how likely it is to be rolled. **6 and 8** have the most dots, so they come up most often (and are printed in red). 2 and 12 are the rarest.

## Setup: your first two settlements

Each player places **two settlements and two roads** before the game begins. The first player places one settlement and road, then the turn goes around the table, and the last player places their second settlement and road straight away. Then the order reverses back to the first player.

Settlements go on the corners where tiles meet. The **distance rule** says no settlement can be placed next to another settlement: there must be at least two road lengths between them. Your second settlement earns you one of each resource from the tiles around it.

## Taking a turn

1. **Roll for resources.** Every player with a settlement next to a tile showing the rolled number collects one of that resource (two for a city).
2. **Trade.** Trade with other players, or with the bank at 4 to 1. A **harbour** improves your bank rate to 3 to 1, or 2 to 1 for one specific resource.
3. **Build.** Spend resources on roads, settlements, cities and development cards.

## Building costs

| Build | Cost | Victory points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road | 1 brick, 1 lumber | 0 |
| Settlement | 1 brick, 1 lumber, 1 wool, 1 grain | 1 |
| City | 2 grain, 3 ore (upgrades a settlement) | 2 |
| Development card | 1 ore, 1 wool, 1 grain | Varies |

## The robber and the 7

There's no tile numbered 7, but 7 is the most common roll. When someone rolls a 7:

1. Every player with **more than 7 resource cards** discards half of them, rounded down.
2. The roller moves the **robber** to any tile. That tile produces nothing while the robber is there.
3. The roller steals one random card from a player with a settlement or city on that tile.

## Development cards

- **Knight:** move the robber and steal a card. Play three knights to claim **Largest Army** (2 points).
- **Victory point:** worth 1 point. Keep it hidden until you win.
- **Road building:** build two free roads.
- **Year of plenty:** take any two resources from the bank.
- **Monopoly:** name a resource, and every other player gives you all of theirs.

## Bonus points

- **Longest Road:** the first player with an unbroken road of at least **five segments** gets 2 points, until someone builds a longer one.
- **Largest Army:** the first player to play **three knights** gets 2 points, until someone plays more.

## Winning

The first player to reach **10 victory points** on their turn wins.

## Strategy tips

- **Spread your numbers.** In setup, choose spots that touch high-probability numbers (5, 6, 8, 9) and don't rely on a single number.
- **Brick and lumber early, ore and grain late.** Roads and settlements need brick and lumber. Cities and development cards need ore and grain. Plan for both.
- **Grab a harbour** if you produce a lot of one resource.
- **Cities double your income.** Upgrading is often better than building new settlements once space is tight.
- **Trade generously early.** Players remember who's easy to deal with, and the game moves faster.
- **Hide your strength.** Hidden victory point cards help you avoid becoming the robber's favourite target.
