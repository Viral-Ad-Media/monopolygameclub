Ask five families how Monopoly works and you'll get five different answers. Most of us learned from older siblings, and over the years "rules" like money on Free Parking crept in. This guide covers the **official rules** from the standard edition so you can settle the argument.

## Setting up

- Each player picks a token and puts it on **GO**.
- One player is the **Banker**. The Banker can also play, but keeps the bank's money separate from their own.
- Every player starts with **$1,500**: two $500s, two $100s, two $50s, six $20s, five $10s, five $5s and five $1s.
- Shuffle the Chance and Community Chest cards and place them face down on their spaces.
- Everyone rolls both dice. The highest roller goes first, and play moves to the left.

## Taking a turn

Roll both dice and move your token clockwise that many spaces. What happens next depends on where you land. If you roll **doubles**, take your turn as usual and then roll again. Roll doubles three times in a row and you go straight to jail.

Every time you **land on or pass GO**, collect $200 from the bank. Landing exactly on GO does not pay double. That's a house rule.

## Buying property

When you land on an unowned property, you can buy it from the bank for the price printed on the board.

If you don't want it, the Banker **auctions it** immediately. Every player can bid, including the player who declined, and bidding can start at any price. This is the most commonly skipped official rule, and it makes a big difference. Auctions put more property into play sooner, which makes the game faster.

## Paying rent

Land on a property someone else owns and you owe them the rent printed on its title deed. The owner has to ask for it. If they don't notice before the next player rolls, the rent is forfeited.

- Owning **every street in a colour group** doubles the rent on the unimproved streets in that group.
- **Railroads** charge $25, $50, $100 or $200 depending on how many the owner has.
- **Utilities** charge 4 times the dice roll if the owner has one, or 10 times if they have both.
- No rent is due on a **mortgaged** property.

## Building houses and hotels

Once you own a complete colour group, you can buy houses from the bank on your turn or between other players' turns. Two rules apply:

1. **Build evenly.** You can't put a second house on any street in the group until every street has one, and so on.
2. **Hotels come after four houses.** To build a hotel, pay its price and return the four houses on that street to the bank.

The bank has only **32 houses and 12 hotels**. When they run out, nobody can build until some are returned. If more than one player wants the last few houses, the Banker auctions them.

## Jail

You go to jail if you land on the **Go to Jail** space, draw a card that sends you there, or roll doubles three times in a row. You don't collect $200 on the way. If you simply land on the Jail space, you're "just visiting" and nothing happens.

To get out, you can:

- **Pay $50** before you roll on your next turn,
- **Use a Get Out of Jail Free card** if you have one, or
- **Roll doubles** on any of your next three turns.

If you fail to roll doubles on your third turn, you must pay $50 and then move by the amount you rolled. While you're in jail you can still collect rent, buy and sell houses, and trade. Our [jail strategy guide](/monopoly-jail-strategy) explains when it's smart to stay put.

## Free Parking

Officially, **nothing happens** on Free Parking. It's just a free resting place. Taxes and fines go to the bank, not to the middle of the board. The Free Parking jackpot is the most popular [house rule](/best-monopoly-house-rules) in the world, and it's a big reason family games can drag on for hours.

## Taxes

- **Income Tax:** pay $200 to the bank.
- **Luxury Tax:** pay $100 to the bank. Older editions charged $75.

## Mortgages

If you need cash, you can **mortgage** an unimproved property to the bank for the mortgage value printed on its deed, which is half its price. You must sell any houses on that colour group back to the bank first, at half what you paid. To lift a mortgage, pay the mortgage value plus **10% interest**.

## Trading

Players can trade property, cash and Get Out of Jail Free cards with each other at any time, on any terms they agree. Buildings can't be traded. They have to be sold back to the bank before a street changes hands. For tips on getting good deals, see our [Monopoly trading strategy](/monopoly-trading-strategy).

## Bankruptcy and winning

If you owe more than you can pay, even after selling buildings and mortgaging property, you're **bankrupt**.

- If you owe another player, you hand over everything you have, including mortgaged property, to that player.
- If you owe the bank, your property goes back to the bank and is **auctioned** to the remaining players.

The last player left in the game wins.

## Quick reference: rules people get wrong

| Common belief | Official rule |
| --- | --- |
| Money goes on Free Parking | Nothing happens on Free Parking |
| $400 for landing on GO | Always $200 |
| Unbought property stays with the bank | It's auctioned straight away |
| You can't collect rent from jail | You can |
| You must go around the board once before buying | You can buy on your first trip |
