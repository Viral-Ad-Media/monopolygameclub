Being sent to jail feels like bad luck, but in Monopoly it can be a gift. Whether you should leave straight away or stay as long as possible depends on one thing: **how dangerous the board is**.

## How jail works

You go to jail if you land on Go to Jail, draw a card that sends you there, or roll doubles three times in one turn. You don't pass GO or collect $200.

On each of your next turns you can get out by:

- **Paying $50** before you roll,
- **Using a Get Out of Jail Free card**, or
- **Rolling doubles**, which moves you out by the amount you rolled.

If you haven't rolled doubles by your third turn, you pay $50 and move. While in jail you can still **collect rent**, build, mortgage and trade. That's the key to the whole strategy. See the full [official rules](/official-monopoly-rules-explained) for more.

## Early game: get out fast

At the start of the game, most property is still unowned and there are few houses on the board. Every turn you spend in jail is a turn you're not buying property, and other players are.

- **Pay $50 or use your card straight away.** Fifty dollars is cheap compared to missing out on a street.
- Leaving jail sends you toward the **orange and red groups**, which are among the best buys on the board.

## Late game: stay in as long as you can

Once your opponents have houses and hotels, every roll is a risk. In jail you can't land on their property, but you still collect rent from anyone who lands on yours.

- **Don't pay to leave.** Roll for doubles and hope you miss.
- **Keep your Get Out of Jail Free card** to trade or use later.
- Three turns in jail can save you hundreds, or thousands, of dollars.

## The in-between

In the middle of the game, ask yourself:

1. **Is there still property I want to buy?** If yes, get out.
2. **Is the board ahead of me dangerous?** The stretch after jail (pink, orange, red and yellow) is often where players build first. If those groups have three or more houses, stay in.
3. **Do I need to pass GO for cash?** If you're short of money, getting moving might be worth it.

## Get Out of Jail Free cards

These cards can be traded like any other asset. Early in the game they're worth about $50. Late in the game they're worth less to you, because you'll want to stay in jail, but they're still a useful sweetener in a trade.

## Summary

| Stage | Board | Best move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early | Lots of unowned property | Pay or use your card to leave at once |
| Middle | Some sets with a few houses | Depends on the danger ahead |
| Late | Houses and hotels everywhere | Stay in and keep collecting rent |
