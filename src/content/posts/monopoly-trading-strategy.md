In most games, nobody lands on every street of a colour group by luck. Sets get completed through trades, so the player who negotiates best usually wins. Here's how to make deals that help you more than the other person.

## What you can trade

Under the [official rules](/official-monopoly-rules-explained), players can trade **property, cash and Get Out of Jail Free cards**, in any combination, at any time. You can't trade houses or hotels. They must be sold back to the bank before a street changes hands. Mortgaged property can be traded, but the new owner has to pay 10% interest straight away (and can choose to lift the mortgage at the same time).

## Value properties by what they do, not by their price

A street's printed price says little about its value in a trade. Ask:

- **Does it complete a set for someone?** A street that completes a colour group is worth far more than its price to the person who needs it.
- **How cheap are the houses?** Orange and light-blue sets can be built up quickly. Green and dark-blue sets need a lot of cash.
- **How often is it landed on?** Orange and red streets get more traffic than most.

## The golden rule: complete your set first, or both at once

The most dangerous trade is one that completes a set for your opponent but not for you. If you must hand someone a set, make sure you get one back in the same deal, and ideally a set that you can build on faster.

## Make it look fair, make it favour you

- **Add cash to balance a swap.** If you trade a red street for an orange one, offering $100 on top can make the deal feel fair while you still come out ahead.
- **Think about who can afford to build.** If you both complete a set but your opponent has no money for houses, you win the trade.
- **Ask for a little extra.** A railroad or a Get Out of Jail Free card as a sweetener adds up.

## Three-way trades

When three players each hold a piece of another's set, a three-way trade can give everyone a set at once. Make sure yours is the strongest of the three, or that you're the one with cash to build.

## Trades to avoid

- **Helping the leader.** Never make a trade that gives the strongest player another set, even if the price looks good.
- **Panic trades.** If you're short of cash, it's often better to mortgage than to sell a key street cheaply.
- **Breaking your own sets.** Selling one street from a set you've already built on means selling all your houses too.

## Immunity and future deals

Many groups allow deals like "no rent on my railroads for three turns". These aren't in the official rules, so agree before the game whether they count. If they're allowed, they're a cheap way to sweeten a trade without giving up property. See our list of the [best Monopoly house rules](/best-monopoly-house-rules) for more.

## A quick checklist before you say yes

1. Does this give me a set I can build on now?
2. Does it give them a set? Which one is better?
3. Who has the cash to build first?
4. Is the leader helped by this?
